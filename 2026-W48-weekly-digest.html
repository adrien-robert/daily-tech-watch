Stories of the week

A patching week: WordPress 7.1.5, PHP and Ghostscript

What happenedWordPress 7.1.5 shipped on 18 Nov with a long list of security fixes, including an unauthenticated privilege escalation through Template Part block attributes that could lead to remote code execution. PHP released patched versions on 19 Nov. On 22 Nov pwn.ai showed a Ghostscript flaw (CVE-2026-125396, fixed in 10.08.0) that can run commands on a server through crafted uploads, including public forms in the Forminator and Essential Addons for Elementor plugins.

Where it standsAs of 27 Nov, the 7.1.5 release post is unchanged on the key points: it recommends updating immediately and says backports to older branches ship as they become ready. The Ghostscript report still says no attacks have been reported. Source

Why it matters: Updating WordPress is only part of the job: PHP and Ghostscript sit at host level, so ask your hosts and check they are done on every client site.

  • Ghostscript sandbox escape reaches servers through WordPress upload forms · Mon 23 Nov edition
  • PHP 8.6 released with stricter session security defaults · Mon 23 Nov edition

A plugin backdoor is stopped by WordPress.org's release hold

What happenedOn 22 Nov an attacker used a developer's own WordPress.org account to push backdoored releases of six WP connect plugins, including Air WP Sync and WP Sync for Notion. Wordfence rated it 9.8 (critical). The releases never reached sites: they were held by the six-hour delay on plugin updates and blocked by WordPress.org's automatic check.

Where it standsAs of 27 Nov, The Repository reports that the Plugins Team says Protect the Shire has blocked more than 100 releases with severe security issues since September, and that all six plugins were closed on WordPress.org the same evening. Source

Why it matters: None of the six plugins is in your stack, but the safeguard only covers plugins hosted on WordPress.org. Keep a close eye on auto-updates for plugins sold elsewhere and keep backups current.

  • Compromised developer account backdoors six WP connect plugins, stopped by a release hold · Tue 24 Nov edition
  • WordPress.org's Protect the Shire has blocked over 100 severe plugin releases since September · Fri 27 Nov edition

WordPress 7.2 heads for 8 Dec, with /random/ switched on

What happenedWordPress 7.2 reached Release Candidate 3 on 24 Nov, and core confirmed it is compatible with PHP 8.6 while keeping the minimum at PHP 7.4. The release adds three read-only core abilities for content, users and settings. The /random/ redirect will ship on by default despite developer pushback.

Where it standsAs of 24 Nov, the RC3 announcement still gives 8 Dec 2026 as the release date and says RC3 includes performance work on the random-content redirect for larger sites. Source

Why it matters: You have about ten days to test your Bricks, ACF, WPML and SEOPress setups on staging, and to decide whether client sites should expose /random/ (the wp_enable_random_content_redirect filter turns it off).

  • WordPress 7.2 will ship the /random/ redirect switched on by default · Tue 24 Nov edition
  • WordPress 7.2 is fully compatible with PHP 8.6, minimum PHP stays at 7.4 · Tue 24 Nov edition
  • WordPress 7.2 Release Candidate 3 is out, final release scheduled for 8 December · Wed 25 Nov edition
  • WordPress 7.2 adds three read-only core abilities for content, users and settings · Wed 25 Nov edition

A Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann hypothesis is confirmed

What happenedLevent Alpöge published a proof on 20 Nov as 17 million lines of Lean code, found working with Claude. On 22 Nov the Lean and Mathlib community said it has full confidence the proof is correct, after dozens of independent checks.

Where it standsAs of 22 Nov, The Decoder reports the confirmation stands, and that forecasters had expected this milestone around 2035. Source

Why it matters: Nothing to do, but it is the week's strongest evidence of what current AI models can do on research-level problems, and clients will ask about it.

  • Lean community confirms Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann hypothesis · Mon 23 Nov edition
