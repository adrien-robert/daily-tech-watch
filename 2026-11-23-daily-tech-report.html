Quad9 stops answering DNS queries in France on 24 Nov

⏰ Deadline

According to Numerama, Quad9 will stop responding to users in France on 24 Nov 2026, choosing to withdraw rather than block 58 sites under a court order. Anyone who set Quad9 as their DNS resolver and has no other one will lose access to websites.

Why it matters: Check your office router, your machines and any client or hosting setup that uses Quad9 as its DNS, and switch to automatic DNS or another provider before tomorrow.

via Numerama · published Sun 22 Nov

Lean community confirms Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann hypothesis

🎯 Milestone

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge published on 20 Nov a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, found working with Claude, as 17 million lines of Lean code. On 22 Nov the Lean and Mathlib community said it has full confidence the proof is correct after dozens of independent checks.

Why it matters: Nothing to do, but you should know: it is the strongest public evidence so far of what current AI models can do on research-level problems.

via The Decoder · published Sun 22 Nov

Microsoft says attackers exploited a flaw in Entra Connect Health to read tenant data

🔒 Security

Microsoft is notifying organizations that an API flaw in Entra Connect Health, introduced on 23 Jul and found on 9 Nov, was exploited to read tenant metadata including administrator email addresses. Microsoft fixed it server-side on 19 Nov and says no passwords, tokens or configuration changes were involved.

Why it matters: If any client runs Microsoft 365 with Entra, expect targeted phishing aimed at their administrators, and make sure those accounts use phishing-resistant MFA.

via BleepingComputer · published Sat 21 Nov

Bending Spoons buys Mailchimp from Intuit for $3.68B

🆕 New

Intuit is selling Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for $3.68B, about 90% cash and 10% stock, according to TechCrunch. Intuit bought it in 2021 for roughly $12B, and Bending Spoons also acquired AOL, Eventbrite, Airtable and Miro in the past year.

Dragos finds attackers stealing prompts at three AI inference providers

🔒 Security

Dragos seeded about two million prompts with fake credentials from July to November and found three providers where attackers had been copying requests for months, some flagged Zero Data Retention. OpenRouter said on 22 Nov it removed the three providers and took 125 endpoints out of Zero Data Retention eligibility.

Why it matters: Do not send real API keys, client credentials or private data in prompts through third-party gateways or cheap inference resellers, and rotate any key that went through one.

via The Hacker News · published Sun 22 Nov

Ghostscript sandbox escape reaches servers through WordPress upload forms

🔒 Security

Security firm pwn.ai showed that CVE-2026-125396, a use-after-free in Ghostscript fixed in version 10.08.0 on 8 Sep, can run commands on a server via a crafted file sent to upload or preview features. Public upload forms in the Forminator and Essential Addons for Elementor plugins were among the demonstrated paths, and WordPress Core needs an account with upload rights, which Authors and above have. No attacks have been reported.

Why it matters: Ask your hosts whether Ghostscript is at 10.08.0 or later, since several Linux distributions still shipped a vulnerable version. Updating WordPress does not replace this fix, and a copy of Ghostscript bundled in a container is not updated with the host.

via The Hacker News · published Sun 22 Nov

PHP 8.6 released with stricter session security defaults

🆕 New

PHP 8.6 came out on Thursday 19 Nov alongside the patched 8.5.13, 8.4.28, 8.3.37 and 8.2.36. According to The Register, it tightens defaults for the session extension's Strict Mode, HttpOnly and SameSite settings.

Why it matters: Test custom code and plugins that rely on session cookies before any host moves a site to 8.6. For now the priority is still the security releases of your current branch.
